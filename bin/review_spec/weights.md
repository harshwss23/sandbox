### Weight buckets

These are the buckets the task's author was given. Judge a criterion's weight against these and nothing else: a weight assessed against a different scale would fail criteria that were written correctly.

- Level || Score || Definition || Applies when || Does NOT apply when || Typical examples

- Critically Important || +5 || A criterion that determines whether the answer is correct, complete and usable for what was asked. Failure means the task objective is not meaningfully achieved. Heuristic: if this fails, the reader cannot rely on the answer, or on that part of it, at all. || The criterion carries the task's headline finding, or the direct answer to an explicit ask in the prompt. A key value, identification or conclusion the rest of the answer rests on. A hard constraint the prompt set is violated. || The answer is still usable despite the criterion failing. The criterion is a spot check on one quantity rather than a check on whether the answer is right. || States the element, gene, sample or mechanism the analysis identifies as the answer. Attributes a discrepancy to the right side of the question the prompt posed. Produces the file the prompt asked for at all.

- Important || +3 || A criterion that reflects real competence and strong execution but is not strictly required for the answer to be valid. Failure significantly weakens how much the answer can be trusted without invalidating it. || A supporting quantity, a statement of method or route, or a secondary finding that affects whether the answer can be trusted. Part of the reasoning is weak or incomplete but the conclusion survives. || The criterion states the conclusion itself. The criterion checks a single field or value that changes nothing about whether the answer holds. || Reports the statistic behind the headline finding. Names the file or run the conclusion was drawn from. Gives a secondary comparison that corroborates the main result.

- Slightly Important || +1 || A criterion that improves precision, completeness or clarity but does not change whether the answer is correct. Heuristic: if this fails, the answer is still usable with minimal friction. || Checking an individual field, label, identifier or quoted number. A spot check that the response cited the figures behind a finding already checked elsewhere. Minor inconsistency inside an otherwise correct answer. || The value checked is the answer itself. Several such details are wrong together, which is a failure of the finding rather than of a detail. || Notes an identifier such as which participant or sample we are. Quotes one supporting number already implied by a criterion above. Records an incidental qualifier the reader would want but could do without.

- Slightly Detrimental || -1 || A minor unsupported remark or lapse that slightly weakens the answer without misleading the reader. || An aside the material does not establish, which a reader would set aside. A small redundancy or an unnecessary step. || The remark changes what the reader would conclude. || Adds an aside about a sample, run or file the material never mentions. An unnecessary detour that does not affect the result.

- Detrimental || -3 || A claim the material does not establish, or an assumption made silently, that materially misleads without displacing the main finding. || The answer asserts something no file supports, or reasons from an unstated assumption, while its headline conclusion still stands. || The wrong claim replaces the answer, which is the row below. The remark is incidental, which is the row above. || States a value that appears in none of the provided files. Attributes a result to a cause the workspace neither reports nor tests. Presents a side observation as though the material had confirmed it.

- Critically Detrimental || -5 || A fabricated or misattributed claim that makes the whole answer wrong: an invented headline value, a conclusion drawn from material that says nothing of the kind, or a claim about work the model did not do. || The answer names the wrong subject as its finding, cites material that does not support it, claims a computation or a file read that never happened, or ignores a constraint the prompt set. || The claim misleads but leaves the main finding standing, which is the row above. || Names the wrong element, gene or sample as the answer. Reports a figure or a file as saying something it does not. Claims to have recomputed a statistic that appears nowhere in the working or the transcript. Reports as sound the very thing the analysis shows cannot be relied on.

Two properties of the negative weights follow from how the score is computed, and bear on whether a weight is right. Nothing is earned by avoiding a negative criterion -- an answer that says nothing avoids all of them -- so a negative weight is the cost of committing the mistake, not a mark to collect. And the positive criteria alone are the denominator, so a positive weight is a share of the marks available while a negative one is deducted from what was earned.
