### Naming, across the criteria and against the material

You are given the prompt and a listing of the workspace as well as the criteria. They are here for the names the task's own material uses and for nothing else. Ask of each criterion whether a grader would know which thing it is about.

- **Against the material.** The criterion names its subject by the name the prompt or the workspace gives it -- a file, a column, a quantity, an entity. A criterion that invents a name for something the material already names leaves the grader to guess the mapping.

- **Across the criteria.** One thing carries one name in every criterion that mentions it. Where two criteria name the same thing differently, a grader cannot tell whether they are about one thing or two, and the rubric reads as covering more than it does.

- **By position.** A criterion pointing at "the second", "the other" or "the remaining" one of something names a place in a sequence it never states, and which one is meant depends on how the reader happened to order the material.

A mismatch is a finding only where it changes a verdict, and this is the half to hold to: a synonym a reader of the field resolves without hesitating is not a finding, however far it sits from the prompt's own wording. Report it where the name leaves two candidates in the material or none, and say which candidates. Where the name admits a second reading the category is Dense or Ambiguous Phrasing; where the criterion's target is absent altogether it is Criteria Not Self Contained. Reading the prompt here does not make a criterion that needs the prompt in hand a self-contained one.
